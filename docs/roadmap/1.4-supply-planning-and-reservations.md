# 1.4: supply planning and paid reservations

Status: implemented (code) — pending migration 005 + concurrency proof. Target: Oct 22-24. Effort: 12-16 focused hours. Depends on: 1.1-1.3. Next: [1.5](1.5-paypal-lifecycle-and-reconciliation.md).

**Outcome:** FarmBroker selects a feasible supply plan and commits stock exactly once after payment, without selling the same kilograms to two buyers. First demonstrate two paid orders competing for the same inventory without over-allocation.

## Implementation sequence

1. Define the supply snapshot and lot constraints: crop/specification, on-time availability, allocatable quantity, quality eligibility, settlement unit price, and accepted quote budget.
2. Build a small deterministic candidate generator: cost-first and quality/reliability-first; diversification is optional. Evaluate every candidate with the same validator. Ask the 1.2 model to select among valid candidates and explain the tradeoff.
3. Add reservation records and one atomic Postgres reserve operation that locks supplier/lot rows in a stable order, rechecks availability, and writes allocations plus deal state.
4. Persist per-lot contractual payout rates and plan versions. Execution uses the accepted contract rather than recomputing unrelated supplier prices.
5. Add invalidation/resume and reservation release. Carry replacement capacity information into 1.6 without reserving unpaid speculative supply.

## File, data, and API targets

Extend `src/lib/agents/procurement.ts`, `agents/pricing.ts`, `api/procure`, and Hub supplier handling. Add `src/lib/procurement/plans.ts`, `validate.ts`, `reservations.ts`. Suggested migration `005_supply_reservations.sql`: `supply_reservations`, allocation attempt/version fields, contractual unit rates, and a transaction/RPC such as `reserve_paid_plan`.

Retain supplier rows as the inventory source for the minimum release. Add a separate `supply_lots` table only if one supplier genuinely needs multiple dated/specification lots. Retrying a rejected supplier must use a new allocation attempt rather than overwriting inspection history; revise the current unique `(deal_id, supplier_id)` constraint accordingly.

## Implementation checklist

- [ ] Use persisted request crop, pool/scenario, specification, deadline, and accepted quote; no query-string substitution.
- [ ] Calculate available quantity as current offer quantity minus active commitments. Accepted/transferred quantity stays consumed; rejected or cancelled quantity follows an explicit release/quarantine rule.
- [ ] Exclude ineligible/late/quarantined lots and zero remaining quantities before candidate generation.
- [ ] Require complete target quantity, affordable supplier entitlement, and allowed modeled costs/reserve for a valid guaranteed plan.
- [ ] Compare at least two feasible strategies when alternatives exist; one feasible candidate is acceptable when supply is constrained.
- [ ] Keep candidate calculations deterministic and versioned. The model selects a valid plan ID rather than inventing allocation arithmetic.
- [ ] Treat “reliability score” as a demo heuristic unless backed by observations; show the contributing fields and missing-data policy.
- [ ] Preserve the legacy happy-path allocation and clearing-rate settlement math in its own fixture.
- [ ] Record floor-cost estimates separately from contractual supplier payouts; do not mix min-price allocation costs with clearing-rate settlement liabilities.
- [ ] Persist plan snapshot, supplier/lot versions, allocated kg, payout rate, policy version, and validated cost total.
- [ ] Require verified paid status and an accepted quote for reservation execution. Prepayment analysis/acceptance creates zero commitments.
- [ ] Reserve all rows atomically or return a conflict. Do not leave half a plan locked after a failed insert.
- [ ] Serialize competing reservations with Postgres row locks and check remaining quantity inside the transaction. Do not hold locks while calling the model or PayPal.
- [ ] Protect retries with a unique operation ID; replays return the stored operation result.
- [ ] Re-read current facts after a stale-plan conflict, rerun validation, and choose again within bounded attempts.
- [ ] Persist the chosen reservation/allocation before showing “Supply locked”.
- [ ] Release only the affected reservations on rejection/cancellation, with audit events. Never release accepted platform-owned quantity as if unused.
- [ ] Add authorized Hub views for committed/remaining stock and internal plan comparison; buyer sees target/allocated/accepted/remaining aggregates.
- [ ] Keep a simulated standby lot available in the recovery fixture. Its existence and activation conditions are explicit, not generated after failure to fake coverage.
- [ ] Mark paid-but-uncoverable orders as unresolved/recovering for 1.6. Do not mark completed or retroactively increase the buyer charge.
- [ ] Update procurement API, schema, and agent documentation, including concurrency and release semantics.

## Acceptance and verification

1. Two paid orders concurrently request 10,000 kg from a shared 10,000 kg pool. At most one receives the full reservation; total committed quantity never exceeds stock. The other receives a recoverable shortage/conflict state.
2. Replay a reserve operation ten times. Allocation quantity, reservation count, and events remain unchanged after the first success.
3. Inject a DB failure mid-reservation. No partial plan or mismatched request/deal state persists.
4. Change availability after a quote or AI selection. Commit revalidation prevents stale/late/over-budget supply from being reserved.
5. An unpaid request can evaluate candidates but creates no reservation, allocation, supplier commitment, or settlement liability.
6. Inspect/reject a lot, then retry a new eligible attempt. The original inspection remains readable and stock accounting remains conserved.
7. Verify buyer JSON contains no supplier identities or internal cost data; Hub plan comparison contains sufficient evidence to understand the choice.

Use an isolated Postgres/Supabase test workspace for actual concurrency checks; a pure-function unit test cannot prove the row-lock invariant.

## Scope cut and exit gate

Minimum: two simple strategies, hard validation, per-supplier inventory, atomic reservations, version checks, and retry/release behavior. Cut optimization solvers, geography routing, multi-lot modeling, historical prediction, and elaborate diversity scoring.

**Exit gate:** every paid kilogram is accounted for, retries do not duplicate commitments, and the chosen AI plan is tied to executable inventory and contract facts.
