# Competitive context and defensible differentiation

Status: positioning hypothesis, not a world-first claim or a complete market study. Checked October 15, 2026.

## What the initial comparison changes

Tridge publicly describes AI-backed agri-food procurement, evidence-grounded decisions, supplier-network execution, supply-risk response/alternative sourcing, and grower-allocation/stock modules. Its public AX page also describes human approval gates for external communication/status changes. Those are the vendor's published descriptions; they are not independent validation of every deployed behavior. [Tridge AX](https://www.tridge.com/tridge-ax)

Therefore, “AI for agricultural sourcing,” aggregation, evidence-backed planning, and alternative sourcing are insufficient originality claims on their own.

## A narrower hypothesis for FarmBroker

A small domestic herb-procurement operator can offer a **pre-authorized, financially bounded outcome contract**: the buyer confirms terms and pays, then AI decisions execute within deterministic limits, repair an inspection shortage automatically, and retain an inspectable link between money, stock, accepted obligations, and final outcome.

We can demonstrate that combination on the actual sandbox product. We cannot infer that another company lacks the same capability because its public page does not document it.

| Dimension | FarmBroker 2.x target | Evidence to earn the claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Domestic bulk-herb buyer and collection Hub; farmer benefit is secondary through aggregation/clear entitlement | Consented buyer/operator discovery and prototype tasks |
| Authority | Prior contract/policy allows specific automatic execution/repair | No extra approval click within permitted recovery; cap/spec changes blocked |
| Payments | Actual PayPal sandbox funds gate commitments and support full compensation | Capture/refund proof linked to execution |
| Decision trace | Selected model plan is bound to quoted/committed stock | Run/plan/quote/receipt hash chain |
| Failure response | Accepted stock remains fixed; only the shortage is repaired | Minimal-repair versus full-rebuild results |
| Economics | Buyer charge fixed, actual supplier obligations preserved, margin/exposure explicit | Per-lot entitlement and budget invariants |
| Operability | Small native infrastructure with a documented supported workload | Load/fault/cost artifacts on the named tier |

This is a testable differentiation proposal, not a claim to outperform Tridge's data/network/logistics. FarmBroker currently has synthetic inventory, simulated physical operations, and no validated customer base.

## Competitive research checklist

- [ ] Interview the intended buyer about current broker, marketplace, spreadsheet, and procurement-software alternatives.
- [ ] Compare the same task and failure scenario, not generic feature counts.
- [ ] Add at least two directly relevant alternatives using their current primary documentation.
- [ ] Mark undocumented capabilities unknown, not absent.
- [ ] Separate an implemented behavior from a vendor marketing statement and our own proposal.
- [ ] Test whether prior-authority recovery and clear financial exposure matter to users.
- [ ] Explain adoption, supply verification, logistics, capital, and payout gaps.
- [ ] Avoid claiming a moat from publicly known frameworks/queues/cryptography.
- [ ] Retain negative evidence if users prefer a simpler operator-driven workflow.

Our durable advantage would have to come from verified domain data, reliable execution, user trust, and learned operational policies. Those require work and pilots beyond the current hackathon demonstration.
